1740QCA Motion Design · Assignment 02 · Trimester 2 2026

Motion graphic design

A Process Journal documenting the design of a 2D kinetic text sequence, its extension into 3D text with virtual cameras and virtual lights, and the refinement and compositing of both into a finished motion design synchronised to a soundtrack.

The text is an original twelve-line poem in six rhyming couplets, moving from concealment to disclosure — building walls, the walls breaking, and what grows afterwards. Almost every line contains a physical verb, so the motion design is dictated by the language rather than applied to it: the line about breaking physically breaks, and the line about growth scales. The soundtrack is a cinematic instrumental at 89 BPM, and every animation event is synchronised to a marker placed by ear against the audio waveform.

Reflective Report

The strongest outcome is the final composited piece rather than any single experiment. Extruding the type into 3D text gave the letters weight, and the dark blue background layer, the animated virtual light travelling across the surfaces, and the glow coming off the bevelled edges all amplified what the poem was already saying. The emotion is carried by the depth, not by the movement.

What surprised me is that this was not true at first. My earliest 3D test — simply making the text 3D and adding a spotlight — disappointed me. I had expected dimension to add feeling automatically, and instead found the simpler 2D kinetic text was already doing that job on its own. Only once the virtual camera, material options and glow were working together did the 3D justify itself. A technique does not carry meaning by being present; it has to be developed until it does.

Virtual cameras were the hardest thing to learn. Understanding how position, orientation and point of interest coordinates relate to what the camera actually sees took longer than anything else in the assignment, and I worked by trial and error for much of it. Rendering was also slow, which I solved by disabling shadows while working and re-enabling them before export.

The decision I am most confident about is leaving "I stood there on my own" completely still. The stillness is a play on the words — the line sits by itself, unanimated, and says more than motion would have.

I placed every marker by ear, replaying the track until each one sat where I actually heard the accent, and every key frame is synchronised to those. What I still cannot do is control a camera and a light in the same scene without losing one to the other.
